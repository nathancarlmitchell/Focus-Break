Focus Break - what's left, and what's in.

DONE
	Pause; touch play; Focus shrinks the hitbox and centres it on the cursor; one volume and one
		playback rate drive all eleven tracks at once
	Options: a settings screen of its own, off the start screen, remembered in localStorage.
		Effects (auto/full/reduced/off), Steering, Buttons
	Touch steering ratio: TOUCH_GAIN was hardcoded at 1.25; it is a setting now, 0.75x to 2x
	Touch button side: the power buttons and the pause icon sit against the left or right edge.
		The level name, the grid's gutter and the graze blade all follow them across
	Scoreboard: the top stripe fills as the score walks toward the level's limit
	Graze system: a pass banks charge for how close it was. Charges have three uses - held, each
		adds 25% to the rate; a Break spends one instead of costing 250; and Focus burns one to keep
		scoring while it is slowed, which is the only one of the three you press on purpose. Charges
		carry into the next level but not past a death. Measured in the game, not the effects,
		because it pays
	Visual flare: graze sparks and their particle emitters, the vaporwave horizon grid, per-level
		palette drift, CRT scanlines and a rolling bar, powerup pickup rings, the Break shockwave,
		the score-hundreds flourish, the level-name typewriter and its glitch-out, the big-block
		telegraph, the death shatter, the between-levels transition
	Mobile text: the death and between-levels messages, the settings screen and the pause panels are
		fitted to the band they actually fill instead of to the 1280x800 frame, so a phone stops
		showing them at half the size it has room for. Every line is centred for real
	Mobile frame rate: draw once a frame rather than once a step, and bake the reduced-motion glow
	Difficulty, picked on the start screen because it defines a run rather than configures one. Four,
		on two levers: EASY has three lives, NORMAL is the game as it was, HARD rations power
		activations to three a level, TRUE gives none at all. Holding a power is free; starting one
		costs, including a fallback onto one still held. The HUD says what is being rationed
	Lives: a death spends one and keeps the level's score and the graze bank, so it reads as a level
		transition rather than a reset. Out of lives, a death wipes the score as it always did
	Continuous audio: nothing silences the album any more. A pause used to stop the playing track and
		four sound effects with it, and the level 9 approach turned every song's volume to zero for
		its siren. The pause stops nothing at all now, and level 9 ducks the music to 0.07 under the
		siren's 0.3 rather than muting it, back up when the level starts. The only thing a pause
		still does to the audio is put the playback rate back to 1, since a track left pitched down
		by Focus or up by Warp would drone at that speed for the whole pause
	CLASSIC button on the start screen, launching the 2019 build that has been sitting in the repo
		under CLASSIC EDITION all along. A new tab where the browser allows one, this tab where it
		does not, which is what an embedded frame usually means. It leaves this page rather than
		running inside it, so on touch it gets the free top right corner rather than a fourth seat in
		the menu row
	Instructions: two pages, CONTROLS and SCORING, off a HELP button on the start screen and out of a
		pause - H on a keyboard, a button on the touch pause panel. The wording follows the input
		mode and the button-side setting, so it says what this player actually has. One page would
		have had to shrink to fit a phone, which is the opposite of the point
	Menu glitches: a piece of the start screen tears, ghosts or gets crushed for a moment, then is
		drawn clean. Picks its victim from the title, the slogan, any button, the records line or the
		whole screen. The menu arrives broken and settles - about five of them in the first two
		seconds, easing off over that, then one every five seconds or so. Full effects only, since a
		glitching menu is what reduced motion is asking you not to do, so Effects turns it off
	Records: best run time and what it cost, a best split for each level, and the furthest level
		reached for a run that never finishes. Kept per difficulty, or EASY would set times TRUE
		could never beat. On the start screen, on every cleared-level screen,
		and on the finish screen. Kept beside the settings in localStorage, and nothing is drawn
		until there is something to draw
	Lore cards: a level's story types itself out the way its name does, a few lines under the banner.
		Once per level and never again - a death retypes the name but not the card, since the level
		is being retried and the story has already been told. The words are a placeholder list,
		LEVEL_LORE, sitting next to LEVEL_NAMES to be rewritten. Grey rather than black, so a card
		never reads as something to dodge, and it fades out in every look instead of tearing: the
		tear is the level name's signature and this is the quieter voice. It is fitted into whichever
		of four boxes the layout leaves biggest, because the HUD is a narrow column in mouse play and
		a wide, short block in touch play, so beside the stats and under them are each roomy in one
		layout and useless in the other
	Pre-roll on a level the run has not reached before: the lore card types itself out over an empty
		field, and the level begins when the last character lands. 2.34s at level 1, 3.42s at level 9,
		1.88s at level 15, taken from the card's own text and the typing constants, never from fxLook -
		the look changes how a card arrives, not how long it takes, and the simulation has to be the
		same with the effects off. A death replays the level with no card and no pre-roll: lorePlaying
		decides both, so it is one rule rather than two that have to agree
		Four things stop for it, and each one is load-bearing:
			The spawn clock. Holding it stops the refills, and spawnDue's other half had to move with
				it - that fired on frameNo == 1, so the opening volley would still have landed on the
				first step. It is playFrame() == 1 now, which is that same first step of the level
				rather than of the reading
			The score, guarded once inside addScore. Score is a clock rather than a reward for dodging,
				so leaving it running would not have shortened the level - except under Warp, which
				doubles the rate and would have been completely safe for the whole pre-roll. Three and
				a half seconds of that is a third of a level banked before anything can touch you
			The powers, through canAct. A Focus over an empty field would spend a rationed activation
				on HARD and burn a graze charge for nothing. One held through the pre-roll takes effect
				on the first played step, exactly as one held through a pause does
			levelStart, set when the pre-roll ends rather than when the level does, so the split times
				the level and not the reading. A pause inside a pre-roll holds it and is outside the
				split as well, since frameNo does not advance while paused
		The touch steering hint moved onto the same clock. It showed for frameNo < 200, which a
			234-step pre-roll would have eaten whole on level 1, the only level that shows it

GAMEPLAY STILL TO DO
	Tune the mobile difficulty. Not an illusion - measured, and there are two separate effects, both
		against the player, because obstacle speeds are absolute px per step and never scaled:
			Time to cross the whole field, which is all the warning there is. A fast block takes 4.80s
				on a 1920 window, 3.20s at 1280, 2.11s on an 844-wide landscape phone and 1.67s on a
				667. So a phone gives two thirds of a 1280 desktop's warning and 44% of a fullscreen
				one. "They move across the screen faster" is exactly right: screens per second is
				what changed, and it is the number the player actually reacts to
			Room to dodge into. A 25x25 block is 2.3% of a 1080-tall field and 6.4% of a 390-tall one,
				and the square goes from 1.9% to 5.1%. Nearly three times as much of the screen is
				taken up by the thing you are avoiding and the thing you are avoiding it with
		One thing is already easier on a phone, and it is worth not undoing: the bar pair gap is an
			absolute 85-125px, so on a short screen it is 22-32% of the height against 8-12% fullscreen
		What already scales with the window: bar pair heights, the big blocks (heightScaledBlock takes
			a fraction of the height), the HUD, the grid's gutter. What does not: OBSTACLE_SPEEDS, the
			25x25 and 10x10 blocks, the 10x50 oblongs, the 20px bar width, pieceSize, the bar gap,
			GRAZE_PX, scoreLimit, every spawn rule's `every`, FX_TELL_STEPS. So the fix is a decision
			about which of those become fractions of the window, not a tuning pass on any one of them
	Level 10: add a bonus to the total score; slowly increase spawns as the level goes on
	Break could destroy all obstacles, or just those near the square
	Other difficulty elements: forced hyperspeed, limited ability uses, some hard-mode only
	Increase difficulty on later levels, specifically 14 and 15. 9 and 13 were both harder
		(but maybe that was just me improving)

CODE STILL TO DO
	Separate the code into multiple files. It is one 4477-line HTML file, 217KB, with 4440 lines
		inside a single script tag and 223 top-level functions. The obvious first cut is already there:
		the effects are 1552 of those lines and 62 of those functions, they all start with fx, and they
		only ever read the game, never write to it. Everything before them - state, levels, spawns,
		input, the loop and the screens - is the other 2888.
		Two things constrain how: the game is opened straight off disk as often as it is served, and a
		file:// page can load plain script src files but not ES modules, which CORS blocks. So plain
		scripts and globals, not import/export. That also keeps every test harness working unchanged,
		since they all reach in through window. And the split has to preserve load order - the
		GLITCH_EVERY bug was exactly that, a constant used by an array built above it.
		The artifact publishes supporting files by path, so a split build still publishes as one page
		plus its scripts.

IDEAS, NOT YET PICKED UP
	Seeded runs. The simulation is deterministic: the test harness replaces Math.random with a seeded
		one and gets the same spawns, the same score and the same obstacle positions every time. So a
		run can be named by a seed - a daily one, or a code handed to someone else so they fly the
		same fifteen levels. With the records above that is a leaderboard shape and no server.
		One thing it has to do: seed at the start of the RUN, not at page load. The start screen's
		flashers pull from Math.random on a timer, so a menu that sat for a second has burned about
		55 draws more than one that did not, and the level spawns come out different. Measured
	Endless past 15. SPAWN_RULES is a data table and its `from: 15` rules already have no `to`, so
		they would keep applying. A mode that runs past 15 with `every` tightening as the level
		climbs is a small change to something already table-driven, and gives the records somewhere
		to go once the game is cleared
	Death stats. The graze system measures every pass precisely and then throws it away. The death
		screen could say how many grazes, how close the closest was, how many charges were spent.
		Right now a death is a red number and nothing else
	Per-level score limit. scoreLimit is one global at 1000, so every level is exactly the same
		length. Making it per-level is a one-variable change and a free pacing lever - it would do
		real work on "increase difficulty on 14 and 15" above, which has no mechanism proposed yet
	A powerup that grants a graze charge. The two kinds today are Break and score; a third fits the
		system that was just added
	Obstacle shapes with gaps, rather than solid bars. Changes what dodging means without needing the
		art that "sprites" implies
	Lore on the two victory screens. Neither has any yet, and both are the wrong shape to leave that
		way. What they say today: the level 10 screen is "Victory is a state of mind / 勝利は心の状態です"
		for five seconds over banners, with the split under it; the level 16 finish is "Flawless
		Victory" or "Can you continue?" plus a mistake count, then the time, the best line and the
		restart prompt, and it sits there until the player restarts

			is not the end: five levels follow it, named Perfection or Remains, Afterlife, Desolation,

			Premature congratulation, contradicted a moment later. "You have finished. / 終わりました",

			Name the level it is about to hand over to. Level 10's name already branches on deaths == 0

		Level 16 - the true finish. Terminal, so it can be slower and longer than anything else in the

			practice. / Nothing is chasing you yet.", and the finish is where the yet comes off.
			"Nothing is chasing you." "You can stop now." / "If you want to." "The practice is over.
			The practice is never over." "You may leave the room. / 部屋を出てもよい"
			Branching on how the run got here. Everything needed is already in scope at that point:
				deaths == 0 - "You never once flinched." / "That should worry you."
				deaths == 0 and runBest - "Nobody has done this better. Including you."
				deaths high - "You were wrong twenty-three times and here you are anyway."

RISK, NOT CHECKED
	The game is red and black on white, and red against black is the pair that has to be told apart:
		they ride different lanes, so they move at different speeds. Under protanopia red darkens
		toward black. A player who can't separate them can't predict speed, which is the mechanic
		rather than the paint. Worth simulating before it is a bug report. It may be fine - the two
		also differ in size and behaviour

NOTES ON WHAT IS BUILT
	The effects follow house rules: no red or black, no filled squares, no Math.random (fxHash
		instead), reduced-motion aware, and they read the game without ever changing it. The CRT is
		the deliberate exception: it is a tube over the whole picture, so it uses black and draws
		above the obstacles. The numbers that make that safe are in the file
	Palette drift can move sideways but not down. Red on white is only 3.998:1 and the floor is 3:1,
		so going dark for Afterlife needs the obstacles recoloured first - its own job, not a tuning
		pass. SPAWN_RULES hardcodes black and red in nine places
	The level-name typewriter is English only. The Japanese second line was specced and not built
	Score is not a total. It resets every level, scoreLimit is always 1000, and the finish screen
		reports time and deaths only - so score is a progress bar, and a "score multiplier" means the
		level ends sooner, not that you have more points. Which means it means a better time, which
		only became worth anything once records existed
	What the three graze uses are worth, at the normal 50/sec and a 1000-point level:
		hold one charge for a whole level - 20s becomes 16s, so it saves 4 seconds
		spend one on a Break - saves the 250 points you would re-fly, so 5 seconds
		burn one in Focus - 3 seconds at half rate, about 2 seconds of progress, but flown at a
			quarter of the danger, since Focus slows the world to 0.25x
		Holding and Breaking came out level, which is why neither felt like a decision: spending was
		never worse, so the only strategy was hold until forced. The burn is deliberately the worst
		of the three in raw progress. You are buying safety and paying a premium for it
	A test harness that crashes prints nothing that a word-grep for FAIL or PAGEERROR would catch, so
		it reads as a pass. parts.js was broken for four commits that way: the graze scan moved into
		the game, and it was still calling fxRecord without grazeStep. The runner checks exit codes
		now. It does not check for ": false" - several of these tests report false as the expected
		answer, and that heuristic flagged four passing tests instead
	There is one menu screen variable, not a flag per screen: menuScreen is "", "options" or "help",
		and buttonTable, geom, screenFrame and drawStartScreen all key off it. A new menu screen is a
		band, a button table and a draw function. The instructions are the only one that can come up
		over a paused level rather than over the start screen, which is why closeMenu has to put back
		either one
	setInterval delays in the startScreenIntervals array are read while the script runs, so every one
		of them has to be a value that already exists. GLITCH_EVERY was declared below the array,
		which made it undefined there, which setInterval reads as 0: the glitches rolled about 250
		times a second instead of four and a half for a whole commit. It looked like a design
		problem and was a declaration-order one. Measure the rate, don't read the constant
	Anything on the start screen that runs on a timer must not touch Math.random - it is the stream
		the spawns come from, and how many draws it takes before a run starts then depends on how
		long the menu sat there. The title flasher already does this and burns a few dozen; the menu
		glitches nearly burned a thousand a second before they were moved onto fxHash. The test
		harnesses re-seed at the start of the run for the same reason
	Effects off is the big performance lever on a phone: 60fps at 4x CPU throttle and 45 at 8x,
		against 41 and 19 on full
